About Bihar

A State That Gave the World Its Compass

Long before empires rose and fell elsewhere, Bihar was already writing history. The Mauryan court of Chandragupta Maurya set the standard for governance; the Nalanda Mahavihara defined the ideal of the university; and the Bodhi Tree gave billions of people a path toward inner peace.

Today Bihar is a living museum — where ancient ghats still ring with prayer at dawn, where Madhubani patterns still cover the walls of Mithila homes, and where the same forest that sheltered Valmiki now shelters Royal Bengal tigers.

  • Mahabodhi Temple — UNESCO World Heritage Site, Bodh Gaya
  • Nalanda — the world's first residential university, 5th century CE
  • Chhath Puja — Bihar's singular solar festival, older than history
  • Madhubani art — GI-tagged folk painting tradition of Mithila
  • Valmiki Tiger Reserve — Bihar's sole national park and tiger sanctuary

Culture & Heritage

Bihar in Every Brushstroke

Art, faith, and flavour intertwine here in ways found nowhere else on earth.

🌸

Madhubani Art

Drawn with turmeric and indigo on handmade paper, each Madhubani painting is a coded universe — fish for abundance, lotus for purity, the sun and moon holding all of creation in balance.

Read More

Chhath Puja

Four days of fasting, riverside prayer, and the offering of fruits to the sun god Surya — Chhath is Bihar's most profound festival, celebrated with a fervour that transforms entire cities.

Read More

Valmiki & Wildlife

Named for the sage who wrote the Ramayana within its boundaries, Valmiki Tiger Reserve is Bihar's wild heart — home to Bengal tigers, leopards, Gangetic river dolphins, and over 250 bird species.

Folk Heritage

The Language of Madhubani

🐟

Madhubani painting does not merely decorate — it speaks. Every motif carries a meaning passed down through generations of Mithila women who painted prayers onto walls at dawn. The fish symbolises fertility, the lotus divine purity, the peacock eternal beauty, and the serpent cosmic protection.

Awarded a Geographical Indication tag by the Government of India, Madhubani art now graces the walls of international galleries, the coaches of Indian Railways, and the halls of the United Nations.
